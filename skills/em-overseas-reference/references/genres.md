# Genre cards

These are edited methods from the collected source material, not a current market ranking. Choose the target territory, audience, language, and production conditions from the actual project. A genre label is not a complete story engine.

## T01 — Taboo or forbidden romance

**Core attraction:** desire meets a specific social, professional, familial-role, age, class, authority, or enemy-line constraint. Distinguish unrelated adults occupying family roles from biological relationships. Both leads need independent goals.

**Engine:** attraction → a constraint with real consequences → collaboration or protection → exposure risk → a costly decision → a public or private choice that changes the arrangement. Specify what each person can refuse and what each stands to lose.

Do not substitute a label such as “forbidden” for concrete behavior. A power imbalance, dependency, or debt does not establish consent. Each new encounter should change trust, leverage, or risk rather than repeat flirtation under the same conditions.

## T02 — Rebirth and revenge

**Core attraction:** a previous loss creates a second chance to make a different choice.

**Engine:** establish the original injury → identify the limited window to prevent the first repeat → obtain evidence or resources → break an enemy alliance → let opponents respond → confront changes that make past knowledge less reliable → resolve the chosen injury.

Memory is private knowledge, not public proof. The protagonist needs a route from knowing to acting and proving. Future events should change when interventions matter. Do not make foresight an unlimited answer to every problem.

The source title suggests 60–100 episodes while its example only reaches 60. Treat that as a scope inconsistency, not a completed 100-episode plan.

## T03 — Survival and catastrophe

Choose a dominant rule: infection and factions; disaster with monopolized resources; or the capture/exploitation of people with powers. Combining all three without a hierarchy can obscure the engine.

**Engine:** limited resources → a forced choice → a cost → changed alliances or responsibility → the next survival problem. The protagonist's choices should matter, not merely the arrival of a bigger monster.

A contained base, a radio message, a sound beyond a door, and a resource dispute can carry large stakes. Spectacle is optional; production economy must preserve the cause and consequence of the threat.

## T04 — Magic, status, and the underestimated protagonist

**Core attraction:** dignity and capability challenge a hierarchy that has assigned the protagonist a low place.

**Engine:** test or exclusion → a small expression of power → a resource challenge → a cost or limit → a revealing choice → responsibility at a larger scale. Give the character abilities, habits, or decisions before the awakening; a power upgrade should not create their entire personality.

Define activation, range, limits, price, and exceptions. A love interest cannot solve every obstacle. Shared pain, a magical bond, or a fated connection does not establish consent or remove choice.

## T05 — Child-centered family stories

A child needs a wish, fear, and age/ability-appropriate action. They should change the adults' situation and also receive believable care, recognition, or protection.

Three starting structures: a framed mother leaves with a child; an adopted child seeks belonging; a separated family reunites and must repair the relationship. Reunion does not automatically require a victim to reconcile with an abuser.

For mind-reading or an adult mind in a baby's body, specify who can hear, what can be heard, what the child knows, and what the body can physically do. Child performers or generated children are not automatically cheap or easy to produce.

## T06 — Cyberpunk and future cities

Technology must alter choices and social power. Select the relevant hierarchy: corporate authority; water, oxygen, or lifespan access; body ownership; memory; virtual identity; or black-market permissions.

**Engine:** an excluded person seeks access or freedom → wins a limited permission → attracts institutional attention → pays a price → chooses what identity or dependence to accept. Define who controls the mechanism and what can be challenged.

Neon is a visual feature, not a story engine. An “everything was virtual” reveal should not erase earlier costs without preparation. For detailed extraction from existing material, use `em-worldbuilding` and distinguish rule evidence from purely visual traits.

## T07 — Billionaire and mafia romance

The emotional appeal can involve selective protection, restraint, or the surrender of control—not wealth alone. Distinguish legitimate business authority from criminal power and define what each can actually do.

**Engine:** binding circumstances → a test of trust → a contribution from each lead → an old conflict or feud → a choice about power and control. Preserve both leads' agency. Debt, gifts, protection, or dependence do not equal consent. A powerful person's romantic arc can be visible in what they stop controlling.

## T08 — Fantasy romance

The world rule should create a cost of intimacy: a bond, curse, mortality difference, political obligation, or incompatible need. Both leads need choices and consequences.

**Engine:** attraction under a rule → attempts to live with or evade its cost → a discovery with limits → sacrifice or negotiation → an earned resolution. Establish cures and exceptions; “love fixes everything” is not a substitute for causality.

Distinguish the relationship engine here from T04's capability/status engine. They may combine, but identify which drives the major decisions. Do not repeat the source's claimed popularity rank for fae stories as a current market fact.

## T09 — Mythological systems

Greek, Roman, Norse, and dark religious fantasy can provide recognizable motifs, roles, symbols, and conflicts. Distinguish traditional material, a modern copyrighted adaptation's distinctive expression, and original invention. A familiar deity's name is not a motivation.

Do not equate an actual religion or its followers with villainy. Build the project's specific rule and conflict. A small town, a few meaningful props, and a bounded ritual can convey a large mythic frame without an unmanageable cast or spectacle.

## Combining genres

Ask: **which consequential choice would change if the secondary genre were removed?** If none changes, it may be decorative styling rather than a functioning hybrid. Keep one readable central engine and use the second genre to alter pressure, resources, choices, or payoff.

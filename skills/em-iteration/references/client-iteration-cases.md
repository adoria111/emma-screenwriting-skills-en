## 1. M/M possessive pursuit and tender romance

References: *A Rhapsody for The Crown Prince*, *Royal Blood Mafia Knight*.

**Explicit requirements:** coercive pursuit as core framework, mutual push-pull under identity constraints, possible blended worlds and interesting tension scenes, distinct lead characterizations, no conventionally powerless pursued partner.

**Retain:** forceful pursuit, mutual tension, identity/power barriers, affectionate payoff. **Variable:** distribution of strengths, jobs/species, contract rules, confrontations, external pressure. **Methods:** 01 + 03 + 05 + 08.

**Inference:** the client may want strong emotion with less repetitive one-way oppression/passivity. A capable pursued lead allows counter-leverage. Verify with later feedback.

**Example direction:** a royal dragon ruler depends on a dragon hunter who can remove a flame curse. The hunter has lower status but an irreplaceable ability. Develop why separation is impossible, who yields first, and escalating cost.

**Risks:** verbal defiance without real resources, repetitive imprisonment/escape, coercion without relational development.

## 2. Plus-size heroine's comeback, light comedy, enemies to lovers

References: *Keeping Secrets with the Rowing Captain*, *Dumping My Quarterback After I Slimmed Down*.

**Explicit requirements:** retain enemies to lovers, an outsider heroine defined by a physical or personality-related difficulty, tender romance, optional fantasy. **Retain:** underestimation, romantic reversal, affection, changed self-worth. **Variable:** reason for marginalization, ability, source of hostility, attraction path, fantasy rules. **Methods:** 02 + 03 + 05 + 08.

**Inference:** the desired double promise may be comeback satisfaction plus romantic change, not necessarily weight loss.

**Example:** a werewolf girl mocked for inability to shift competes with a school-team captain. Her unusual perception gradually creates trust and attraction.

**Risks:** attraction begins only after a makeover; bodily difference becomes a moral flaw; no visible enemies-to-lovers turning points.

## 3. Reserved or disabled heir and sunny bodyguard

References: *My Sweet Little Bodyguard Is Built Different* (misspelled in the original brief); *Mr. Pei, Are You Dating the Moon Today?* (descriptive English rendering of a Chinese reference title, not a verified official release title).

**Explicit requirements:** bodyguard/caregiver with a reserved or disabled heir; light comedy; possible M/M or further iteration. The brief also used an autism-related keyword. **Retain:** care/dependence, identity contrast, intimacy from daily interaction. **Variable:** caregiver ability/personality, heir's communication, family pressure, external crisis, gender pairing. **Methods:** 01 + 03 + 04 + 08.

**Inference:** complementarity, care, comic interaction, and intimacy may matter more than fixed genders or a medical condition.

**Example:** a quiet heir and overenthusiastic guard maintain professional distance publicly but depend on each other during danger; the guard has a vulnerability the heir can protect.

**Risks:** conflating quietness, autism, and physical disability; romance as a cure for disability; one-way care without mutual change.

## 4. Retired warrior, status-seeking partner, regret

Reference: *You've Messed with the Wrong Mage* (NetShort, as named in the brief).

**Explicit requirements:** retired powerful warrior, partner chasing status, regret after true identity is revealed, optional gender reversal, close reference pacing. **Retain:** hidden strength, mistaken valuation, revelation, power reversal, regret payoff. **Variable:** gender, ability system, ex-partner's social motives, proof vehicle, punishment. **Methods:** 04 + 07 + 08.

**Inference:** the client may want a lower-risk emotional structure refreshed through gender/setting changes.

**Example:** a retired female war hero abandoned by an ambitious husband retakes military command during a national crisis. Retain underestimated → leaves → authority confirmed → ex-partner regrets, but invent the actual events anew.

**Risks:** implausible motives after gender reversal, scene copying, repeated shock without main-plot movement.

## 5. Endearing child, heard thoughts, rural livelihood

References: *Hearing the Little Alpha's Mind Saved My Life*, *I Spoke Baby Dragon and Won Everyone Over*.

**Explicit requirements:** child with special powers and heard inner thoughts; may combine romance, farming/livelihood, dragons. **Retain:** child ability, knowledge gap, changed adult circumstances, charming contrast. **Variable:** targets (people/animals/dragons/plants), who knows, limits/costs, family/social setting. **Methods:** 06 + 05 + 08 + 03.

**Inference:** the power may function as a portable narrative mechanism connecting romance, household enterprise, and fantasy over time.

**Example:** a child hears a farm guardian dragon's thoughts but cannot directly tell adults. Misread hints avert danger while creating new misunderstandings/crises.

**Risks:** identical thought exposure → universal shock each episode, unlimited power, child as a tool, adults without action/growth.

## Across the cases

Case 1 emphasizes contrast and relational confrontation; 2 outsider identity and enemies to lovers; 3 care and light comedy; 4 gender reversal with preserved emotional rhythm; 5 powers as sustained knowledge asymmetry.

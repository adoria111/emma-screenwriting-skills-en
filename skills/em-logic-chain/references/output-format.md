# Logic-Chain Report Structure

For complete analysis, use the HTML hierarchy: series progress → selected phase's payoff/prerequisites → expandable episode/evidence details. Fields below define coverage, not an inflexible layout. Omit empty thread tables with “no independent thread” where appropriate. Label local-test coverage and unverified parts.

## 1. Coverage and overview

- Scripts/auxiliary sources: files, versions, actual episode/scene coverage, omissions/conflicts.
- Reading status: complete or precise read/unread ranges. A filename saying “complete script” proves nothing.
- Core driver in one sentence: who needs what and why, sustained opposition, loss on failure.
- Main/supporting/opposition/backstory threads: objective, key people, main-plot intersections.
- Phase count/boundary basis: distinguish user choice, script label, auxiliary report, and analytical segmentation. Phase 1 prioritizes explicit first-paywall labeling; later phases use flexible 7–10-episode observation windows. Separate stops from later payoff episodes. Unverified platform placement is not fact.

| Phase | Episode/scene range | Protagonist objective | Actual exit: situation/relationship/knowledge | Dominant pressure | Boundary basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 2. Episode summaries and key information

### Episode N: title by state change

**Core task:** one sentence describing the main change.

**Summary:** compact causes, actions, result, and actual ending. Retain causally necessary events without transcribing every action or merely saying “conflict escalates.”

**Key information:** `fact/rule | who knows or misbelieves | later effect | scene`. Without new facts, explain reinforcement/payoff of established information.

**Emotion:** A's attitude toward B before/after, trigger, behavioral evidence; track B separately when changed.

**Phase function:** assignment, condition supplied, or cost added.

## 3. Per-phase logic (repeat for each phase)

### Phase K | title | episode/scene range

**Payoff and anticipation:** character objective, viewer desire, stopping point, actual payoff episode.

**Entry/exit:** one or two sentences showing the actual state difference. Separate character goal from structural role.

| Prerequisite | Why it supports the endpoint | Key/contributing/gap | How/where source establishes it |
|---|---|---|---|

**Parallel convergence:** each condition has its own setup and converges on the endpoint. Conditions without causal links cannot be chained sequentially.

**Forward chain:** A [episode/scene] → [specific mechanism] B [episode/scene] → [specific mechanism] C → endpoint. Split long chains into main/supporting/opposition paths with convergence nodes. Label inferences; identify chronology or gaps explicitly.

| Person/relationship | Prior feeling/belief | Trigger | Interpretation/need | Emotional change and behavior | Consequence/location |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Explain concrete needs behind turns—survival, belonging, self-respect, duty, control, compensation, revenge—only when supported by behavior/speech. Do not apply psychological diagnoses.

**Other threads:** their actual objectives/actions/convergence/effects. Backstory: past fact → present revelation → present consequence. Do not repeat every episode summary mechanically.

**Handoff to next phase:** new situation/goal, unresolved tension, emotional state, and any external trigger.

## 4. Cross-phase chain

Start with one compact series-wide chain, then explain transitions:

| Prior result/legacy | Next need | Connecting mechanism | Continuing/changing emotion | Evidence and strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Use qualitative evidence labels: explicit causality, enabling condition, inference, external trigger, gap. Do not invent numerical confidence. Use Mermaid only when branching benefits comprehension; retain prose explanations.

## 5. Findings and gaps

Include meaningful findings only: major emotional drivers, unsupported turns, unexplained faction/knowledge changes, delivered/undelivered promises. Give location, textual observation, impact, and inference boundary.

Distinguish a character's lie, conflicting sources, purposeful ambiguity, unstated motivation, and incomplete material. Admit broken chains rather than smoothing contradictions. No issue quota and no unrequested rewrite.

## Appendix: every scene and its evidence

| Episode/scene and presentation order | Goal–action–result | Demonstration/new information and who knows | Emotion/choice | Thread/phase and later function |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover the actual scene count, including establishing shots, repetitions, and flashbacks. Use subitems for independent turns within a scene without inventing original scene numbers. Locate important quotes by original scene and brief excerpt; where absent, pair generated analysis IDs with original locations.

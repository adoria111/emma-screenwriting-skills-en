# Historical Case: Sugar Daddy Project — Chinese-to-US Localization

A historical demonstration of names, cultural markers, and dialogue, not the authoritative source or blanket adaptation permission for a new project. Adjacent reference versions may differ; do not merge them into one canonical draft. General character/motivation redesign belongs to `em-adaptation`. Historical private reference files are not required or bundled.

## 1. Premise

- Source: a family crisis leaves the heroine urgently needing money to save her mother. She signs a contract with a wealthy patron and faces her brother's family's schemes.
- Historical US proposal: a hurricane destroys the parents' income source while the mother's illness creates medical expense. Sarah signs the contract; Jake's family covets the money. Disaster, timing, treatment, insurance, and payment mechanics require project-specific authorization/verification; localization does not automatically add them.

New projects follow approved backgrounds, including plausible Asian names. This table does not authorize whitening characters.

## 3. Candidate cultural markers

Family WeChat conflict → family group chat; transfer/loan → Venmo or wire according to actual function/limits; 110 → 911; lawyer's letter → legal notice of the correct function; Shanghai suburban mansion → a Malibu villa only in the approved setting; Maotai toast → bourbon if appropriate. The source's CNY 800,000 surgery expense cannot simply be converted into an invented US payment requirement: verify condition, insurance, place, amount, and timing.

## 4. Dialogue comparison

Scene: Jake's family pressures Sarah for the patron's money. Source meanings are glossed in English for this edition.

| Source meaning | Literal rendering | Historical adapted example |
|---|---|---|
| Family bonds require helping the brother | “Blood is thicker than water, you can't ignore your brother's trouble!” | “He's your brother, Sarah. Family doesn't turn its back.” |
| Why did family not matter when you abandoned me? | “When you abandoned me, why didn't you think we were family?” | “Funny. You didn't call it family when you left me with nothing.” |
| Why not give money now you have a rich patron? | “Now that you've got a sugar daddy, what's wrong with some money?” | “You're loaded now. Don't act like writing one check kills you.” |
| Refusal of further control | “I won't be controlled by you anymore!” | “Keep the guilt. I'm keeping the money, and my name.” |

The right column contains **free-adaptation examples**, not guaranteed faithful translations. “Left me with nothing” adds a material consequence; “my name” adds identity implications. Use only with supporting facts/permission. Faithful localization must preserve the narrower original meaning instead of copying these additions.

## 5. Reusable lessons

Choose family-pressure wording by register; the English idiom “blood is thicker than water” is not inherently translationese. Long rhetorical questions may become shorter interruptions/subtext when meaning survives. Preserve concrete rescue-money causality without automatically adding disasters. Determine money/register from circumstances. A declaration of independence must remain faithful and character-specific rather than adding a new decision for a memorable line.

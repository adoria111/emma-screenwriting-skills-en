# Hook Mechanisms and Diagnosis

Use to select mechanisms, distinguish weaknesses, and score only when useful/requested. Examples are fictional calibration material, not facts about a real project. Anticipation categories—identity, retaliation, romance, escape, punishment, choice—describe what viewers want, not a fixed hook format.

## Six optional mechanisms

| Mechanism | Existing support needed | Ending can advance | Next episode must address |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imminent payoff | Established goal, obstacle, and waiting | Verification/action actually begins | Answer, result, cost |
| Overturned misbelief | Who misreads what, why evidence is valid | Evidence enters that person's view | Acceptance, reaction, resistance |
| Power transfer | Source/limits of resource, identity, or ability | Old command fails; control changes | New power used, old power resists |
| Relationship change | Existing relationship, desire, motive | Public refusal, choice, allegiance | Response and relationship consequence |
| Unexpected consequence of an existing rule | Rule understood beforehand; supported result | A previously unconsidered condition emerges | Who pays, how they respond; no invented rule change |
| Irreversible action | Established reason and potential loss | Action starts; consequences approach | Completion, cost, or obstruction |

Do not require a different type each episode. When repeating a mechanism, verify progression in action, cost, knowledge, or relationship rather than prolonging one puzzle.

## Five diagnostics and optional scoring

Normally give evidence-backed judgments. For requested scores/comparison, use 0–2 per dimension. Unknown means “unverified,” not zero. Do not calculate a total with incomplete evidence.

| Dimension | 0: clear defect | 1: present but weak | 2: concrete and sufficient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear anticipation | No reason to wait | Question exists, desired result vague | Specific desired outcome/reaction |
| Emotional basis | Outcome detached from predicament | Thin cost/emotional reason | Established situation makes outcome matter |
| Pending consequence | Arbitrary stop/exhausted payoff | Unknown but not imminent | Action brings a specific consequence close |
| Causality | New rule or sudden motive required | Weak setup/link | Existing conflict, evidence, and choice |
| Filmable expression | Only invisible abstraction | Action/dialogue unclear | Change understood without extra exposition |

Scores are editorial judgments, not retention/payment predictions. Fix causality or evidence-boundary failure even if other dimensions score well. There is no mandatory eight-point threshold or full-score loop. Known identity with an unknown opponent reaction can have a strong pending consequence.

## Repair order

1. **Minimal change:** move existing action or tighten the cut; check the next episode does not repeat the event.
2. **Emotional clarity:** strengthen existing reaction, positioning, or choice without changing attachment or core intent.
3. **Visual clarity:** use established objects, evidence, and bodily action. Unsupported glowing props, transformations, or new powers are not merely presentation improvements.

Diagnosis supplies findings/directions without editing. If only the ending may change but the real issue is locked setup/continuation, complete feasible work and identify the dependency. A full-passage revision includes necessary setup and continuation when authorized.

## Calibration

**Viewers know; the mistaken character does not:** viewers saw the heroine win. The host begins announcing the winner. The uncertainty may concern the humiliator's response and transfer of promised business authority, not whether she won.

**Failure may pay off:** a child awaits public acknowledgment; the father arrives but chooses another child. That behavior can answer part of the anticipation and create a new question about the child's next action. Judge only what occurred: hugging another person, silence, or no response yet does not automatically mean explicit rejection or final outcome. Repeatedly keeping the father outside without new resistance is delay.

**Examples do not grant world rules:** a reference crown chooses its ruler by magic. A nonmagical court cannot acquire a glowing crown merely to match it. Existing succession papers/witnesses may create equivalent overturned belief within scope; otherwise flag insufficient support.
